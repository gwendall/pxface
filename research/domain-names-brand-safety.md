# Naming and domain brand-safety audit

Research date: 19 August 2026. Domain availability and prices were checked
through Domani's public `dns-check`, `search`, and TLD catalogue APIs. No domain
was purchased.

## Executive recommendation

The safest naming path is to keep **PXWORD** as the product and font-family
brand, then use a more expressive domain as an alias or eventual canonical URL:

1. **PXWORD — `pxword.page`**: strongest combination of distinctiveness,
   meaning, continuity, and low collision risk. Available through Domani for
   **$17/year** at the time of checking.
2. **PIXLYPH — `pixlyph.com`**: best clean-slate invented brand. The word fuses
   “pixel” and “glyph”, ordinary exact-match web search found no material
   commercial collision, and the `.com` was available for **$16.50/year**.
3. **PXGLYPH — `pxglyph.com`**: precise and distinctive, also clean in
   exact-match web search and available for **$16.50/year**, but less fluid to
   say aloud than PIXLYPH.
4. **PXMONO — `pxmono.com`**: compact, font-like, and available for
   **$16.50/year**. It is semantically narrower: the brand promises a
   monospaced font even if the product later expands.
5. **PXFORM — `pxform.com`**: available for **$16.50/year** and relatively
   collision-free, but less immediately suggestive of type or pixels.

If the goal is an iconic *URL* rather than a legally strong master brand,
**`px.page`** is exceptional: six characters, naturally read as “pixel page”,
available for **$17/year**. The important caveat is that **PX is already an
active technology-company brand at `px.com`**, and two letters are intrinsically
hard to own and search. I would use `px.page` as a memorable product URL or
redirect while retaining **PXWORD** as the name, not rename the font family to
plain “PX”. [PX official site](https://px.com/)

## Collision risk of the obvious roots

This is a practical screening, not a legal clearance opinion. Risk combines
same/adjacent-sector use, search ambiguity, descriptiveness, and likelihood of
confusion.

| Root | Risk | Evidence and implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PXWORD** | **Low** | Invented compound. Exact-match search produced no relevant third-party product; the existing live use is this project. It is much more searchable and defensible than the component `PX`. |
| **PX** | **High as a standalone brand** | `px.com` is an active customer-acquisition technology platform calling itself PX. The term is also the standard abbreviation for CSS pixels, so exclusivity would be weak. [PX](https://px.com/) |
| **PIX** | **High** | Pix is the Brazilian instant-payment scheme created by Banco Central do Brasil and is used by people, companies, government and financial institutions. This is an enormous existing identity even though the sector differs. [Banco Central do Brasil](https://www.bcb.gov.br/en/financialstability/instantpayments) |
| **PIXEL** | **Very high** | Google operates a broad Pixel hardware portfolio spanning phones, watches, earbuds and more. The term is also descriptive for this product. [Google Pixel](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/) |
| **DOT** | **High** | Highly generic and already used by technology companies. Dot Inc. sells tactile and accessibility technology under DOT; `dot.ai` is also an active financial-technology platform. [Dot Inc.](https://www.dotincorp.com/en), [Dot](https://dot.ai/about) |
| **GRID** | **High / weakly defensible** | A basic design and computing term with many unrelated apps and companies. It describes the product's construction rather than identifying its source. Useful inside copy, weak as the master brand. |
| **GLYPH** | **Very high in the relevant category** | Glyphs is a prominent professional font editor; Glyph Design Co works directly in typography and typefaces; an active Glyph product generates brand identity and typography systems. [Glyphs](https://glyphsapp.com/learn/), [Glyph Design Co](https://glyph.co/), [Glyph software](https://glyph.software/) |
| **BIT** | **High in developer tooling** | Bit is an established composable-software platform used by more than 100,000 developers according to its first-party site. It is also a generic computing unit. [Bit](https://bit.dev/) |
| **BLOCK** | **Very high** | Block, Inc. is a large public technology company and explicitly lists BLOCK among its trademarks. [Block trademark notice](https://block.xyz/legal/copyright), [Block company announcement](https://block.xyz/inside/press-release-block) |
| **RASTER** | **High in visual software** | Raster is an active “agent-native” digital asset management product for visual assets, with Figma/API/CDN integrations. That is uncomfortably close to this generator's visual-tool and agent ambitions. [Raster](https://raster.app/) |
| **TYPE** | **High / weakly defensible** | It is the generic category word, while Type.ai is an active AI-first document editor. Great product vocabulary, poor exclusive brand. [Type.ai](https://type.ai/), [Type overview](https://blog.type.ai/post/type-ai-overview) |
| **3X5** | **Medium-high** | It perfectly describes the grid, but the description is shared by many tiny pixel alphabets and at least one already-distributed font family named “3x5”. It can be a model/style descriptor, not a safely unique family name. [Existing 3x5 font listing](https://www.fonttoolbox.com/fonts/3x5) |

The pattern is clear: adding a distinctive second element matters. **PXWORD**,
**PIXLYPH**, and **PXGLYPH** are materially safer than attempting to own `PX`,
`PIXEL`, `GLYPH`, or `TYPE` alone.

### Domani generative pass

Domani's AI suggestion endpoint was also asked for short brandable names. Its
best-looking suggestions did not improve the shortlist after collision checks:

- `px35.app` was available for $23, but **PX35** is already an Opticon hardware
  product/model and reads more like a part number than a font brand.
  [Opticon PX35](https://wiki.opticonusa.com/techsupport/en/PX35)
- `dotfont.app` was available for $23, but **Dot Font** is an existing pixel/LCD
  font distributed since before 2005. [Dot Font listing](https://www.dafont.com/dot-font.font)
- `bitmark.app` was available for $23, but **Bitmark** has several active
  software/crypto uses and a U.S. software-services registration. It should be
  excluded. [Bitmark](https://bitmark.io/)
- `nubfont.app`, `nubtype.app`, `dotset.app`, and `grd35.dev` were available,
  but their meanings or pronunciation are weaker than PXWORD/PIXLYPH.

The endpoint's `hint` invited another iteration with the returned domains in
the exclusion list. A second round was unnecessary because the generated set
was already materially weaker than the manually developed finalists.

## TLD strategy

The TLD should finish the idea rather than merely look fashionable:

- **`.page` — best overall.** It evokes text, a canvas, and publishing without
  limiting the product to font files. `pxword.page` is the strongest safe option;
  `px.page` is the strongest short URL.
- **`.ink` — best typographic metaphor.** `pxword.ink` and `typxl.ink` were
  available, but `px.ink` and `3px.ink` were not. The metaphor is craft-led and
  works for a font brand, though it is slightly less native to a digital pixel
  tool than `.page`.
- **`.zip` — best playful utility metaphor.** `px.zip`, `3px.zip`, `3x5.zip`, and
  `pxword.zip` were available. It makes the URL resemble a downloadable asset,
  which is memorable, but it can also be mistaken for a literal filename. Best
  as a redirect/campaign domain, not necessarily the only production identity.
- **`.graphics` — accurate but less iconic.** `3px.graphics`, `3x5.graphics`,
  and `pxword.graphics` were available. It is longer than the brand and shifts
  attention away from typography.
- **`.design`, `.art`, `.studio` — clear but conventional.** They do not create
  the filename-like domain hack the brief is looking for, and several of the
  shortest combinations were already unavailable.

Domani's current supported TLD catalogue includes `.ink`, `.graphics`,
`.design`, `.studio`, `.art`, `.tools`, `.digital`, `.zip`, `.page`, and many
more. It did **not** list delegated `.font`, `.type`, `.ttf`, `.otf`, `.woff`,
`.pixel`, or `.glyph` options, so constructions such as `px.font` or `px.type`
are not currently purchasable through Domani.

## Recommended decision

For the smallest branding change with the best safety profile:

> **PXWORD**  
> **pxword.page**  
> “A 3×5 pixel type studio.”

For a true clean-slate rename:

> **PIXLYPH**  
> **pixlyph.com**  
> “Make words, pixel by pixel.”

For the boldest domain-led identity, while accepting weaker exclusivity:

> **PXWORD at px.page**  
> Keep `PXWORD` in the masthead and font metadata; use `px.page` as the iconic
> address.

## Legal validation before a rename

WIPO recommends searching both exact and similar-looking/sounding marks,
including the relevant goods/services classes and target countries, before
filing or committing to a brand. Its Global Brand Database spans international
and participating national collections, but WIPO also recommends checking
national/regional registers and notes that database results are not a legal
opinion. [WIPO search-before-filing guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/how_to/search/index),
[WIPO Global Brand Database](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database),
[WIPO terms](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database/terms_and_conditions)

WIPO's terms prohibit automated queries, so this audit did not scrape its
database. Before changing the font's reserved name or investing in a launch,
manually clear the two finalists in WIPO plus EUIPO/INPI/USPTO for, at minimum,
downloadable software/fonts and design/SaaS services, and have counsel assess
phonetic and conceptual similarity. A clean web search and an available domain
are encouraging signals, not trademark clearance.
